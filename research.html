Global Trade Fragmentation, US Tariff Shifts, and SA Equity Valuations

Langa MalimelaChief Research Officer & Executive Director

20 Oct 2026 · 4 min read

The core thesis of fragmentation is complex. Advanced economies face a multi-layered realignment of trade and capital flows, with fragmentation, not globalisation, defining the decade. The transition from a rules-based trading system to a highly fragmented, bilateral transactional model requires a specific cognitive shift.

I. Structural decoupling and emerging market supply chains

South America stands in sharp relief. The regional value chain represents the purpurious reality of international trade in an era of increasing fragmentation. The transition from a rules-based trading system to a highly fragmented, bilateral transactional model requires a specific cognitive shift that market participants have yet to fully absorb.

Market efficiency assumes stable capital flows. Fragmentation invalidates both the transmission mechanism and the timing of the dislocation.— Euclidean Capital Research Desk
